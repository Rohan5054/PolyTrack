# Grounding an RL/Search Agent for PolyTrack: A Literature and Landscape Review

## TL;DR
- The single strongest template for a PolyTrack agent is **Linesight** (pb4 & Agade), which beat 10 of 12 official Trackmania Nations Forever campaign world records in May 2024 using **IQN** (a distributional DQN variant) on discrete keyboard inputs, a reference-line progress reward, and a fast game interface (TMInterface) that gives programmatic input, car-state access, save states, and game-speed-up — exactly the capabilities you must recreate for PolyTrack.
- PolyTrack is unusually well-suited to a **hybrid "learned policy + test-time search"** design because its physics is deterministic, frame-stepped (60 Hz), cheap to simulate, and (since v0.4.0/v0.6.0) implemented in a portable WebAssembly/C++ module — meaning a headless, vectorised, save-state-capable simulator is genuinely feasible, unlike in TrackMania where researchers fight a closed real-time engine.
- Recommended design: build a headless deterministic PolyTrack simulator around the WASM/C++ physics core, train a **discrete-action distributional-RL policy (IQN/Rainbow-style) with a centerline-progress reward and curvature/raycast lookahead observations across procedurally generated tracks for generalisation**, then **per-track fine-tune and run test-time trajectory search (beam search / CEM / MCTS over input sequences)** to convert a good policy into world-record TAS times.

## Key Findings
1. **Prior art in TrackMania is deep and directly transferable.** Linesight (IQN, discrete inputs, reference-line reward) is the state of the art; TMRL (SAC/REDQ, LIDAR observations, rtgym) is the most reusable open framework; Yosh's YouTube project demonstrated an RL agent beating a human world record on A01; and the Sophy-inspired "track-curvature lookahead" (Neinders 2023) is the single most important observation-design idea.
2. **Academic racing RL converges on a small set of choices**: a dense course-progress proxy reward (not sparse finish time), observations in the car-local frame, and explicit lookahead of upcoming track geometry (GT Sophy's course points; Fuchs et al.'s rangefinders). These are what separate "drives without crashing" from "drives an optimal line."
3. **For per-track time optimisation on a deterministic simulator, search can beat pure RL.** Because PolyTrack is deterministic and frame-stepped, trajectory-optimisation methods (CEM, CMA-ES, beam search over inputs, MCTS) and TAS-style segment optimisation can exploit the exact model in ways a reactive policy cannot — mirroring how Yosh combined RL with TAS and how AlphaZero/Stratego combine a learned policy with test-time search.
4. **PolyTrack's technical substrate is favourable.** The game moved physics to WebAssembly (v0.4.0, July 2024) and rewrote the majority of the physics in C++ (v0.6.0, March 2026), enforces determinism (determinism self-test, server-side replay verification), stores runs as compact frame-indexed input recordings at 60 Hz, and exposes tracks as portable base62 "PolyTrack1…" codes plus an in-game editor — an effectively infinite training distribution.
5. **PolyTrack-specific prior art is early and shallow**: PILA (imitation learning from pixels), Polybot (pixel-scraping, failed on timing precision), a manual "TAS_Agent" neural-net experiment on itch.io, the @PolyTAS channel, and beatrixwashere's frame-indexed TAS format + ghost encoder. No one has yet built a headless-simulator, search-augmented RL agent — this is the genuine novelty.

## Details

### 1. Prior art in TrackMania

**Linesight (Linesight-RL/linesight; pb4git & Agade09).** The most advanced public TrackMania AI. It plays *Trackmania Nations Forever* (TMNF), chosen because the game "was designed for equality of input devices which means that keyboard inputs are a viable way to play and therefore that discrete input algorithms like DQN can be applied." Timeline: first human-level driving ~May 2023 (Wirtual played against it in June 2023); in **May 2024 it became the first to beat world records on official campaign tracks — 10 of 12**. Repo README explicitly states "All runs produced by this project are Tool Assisted. They must not be submitted to the Official Leaderboards."
- **Algorithm:** IQN (Implicit Quantile Networks), a distributional Q-learning method. Training uses multiple parallel "collector" (actor) processes feeding a single learner process, each collector attached to its own TMInterface port — an Ape-X-like distributed actor/learner design. A replay buffer, `rollout_results`, `Experience`, and `IQN_Network` are the core objects. Config is hot-reloadable during training.
- **Observation space:** a downscaled grayscale screen image processed by a CNN — the Hall of Impossible Dreams history confirms Linesight "interpret[s] the screen as a 160×120 greyscale" image — plus a scalar `state_float` vector of roughly 59+ entries (speed, velocities, wheel/contact and slide-quality features), and an encoding of the upcoming **reference line**. Agade wrote a custom fast Windows screen-capture library (DXcam) because existing capture was too slow.
- **Reward:** progress along a user-defined reference line — characterised as "the distance traveled along a reference trajectory over the next seven seconds" — i.e., a dense course-progress proxy, not sparse finish time.
- **Interface:** relies on TMInterface (donadigo/Kim) for input injection, car state, screenshots, save states and game speed-up.
- **Reusable for PolyTrack:** the entire IQN + reference-line + distributed-collector architecture is a near drop-in conceptual template. Exact hyperparameters (quantile counts, n-step, PER, action count, wall-clock, GPU) are in the repo config but were not all verifiable in this review — read `config_files/config.py` and the `second_training.html`/`main_objects.html` docs before copying numbers.

**TMRL (trackmania-rl/tmrl; Yann Bouteiller & Edouard Geze, Polytechnique Montréal).** A full distributed RL framework with a Gymnasium environment for TrackMania 2020, built on rtgym (real-time gym for delayed MDPs). Algorithms: **SAC and REDQ**. Observations: either a **19-beam LIDAR** computed from screenshots, or raw images; plus speed. It controls the game with a virtual gamepad (analog) via vgamepad. On the French show *Underscore_* (2022-06-08), the TMRL team pitted a LIDAR policy against TM-Cup 2022 winners Gwen and Affi; per the project's own README, "our policy lost by far (expectedly); the superhuman target was set to about 32s on the tmrl-test track, while the trained policy had a mean performance of about 45.5s" — i.e., robust driving, far from world-record. **Reusable:** rtgym (real-time step timing), vgamepad, tlspyo, and the single-server/multi-client distributed pattern; the LIDAR observation idea maps directly onto raycasts in a PolyTrack simulator.

**Yosh (YouTube).** Trained an RL agent on TMNF's A01. Per Dexerto's reporting, "By the 400-hour mark, the bot was consistently within hundredths of Eddie's world record. Millions of inputs later, the AI pieced together a run that was 0.04 seconds faster than any human" — reaching **23.75 s** solo (beating Eddie's 23.79 s), then **23.72 s** with an auto-drift helper, and finally **23.64 s** after per-segment training and TAS-style brute force surfaced "a tiny misaligned road seam on A01" enabling an earlier downhill jump; Yosh's stated true target was the community TAS of 23.66 s. (Human player Rollin later beat Eddie legitimately at 23.78 s in August 2025.) A follow-up video targeted the A06 world record. The key lesson: **RL got to human-record level; superhuman/TAS level required test-time search and exploiting exact game state** — the central argument for a hybrid design in PolyTrack.

**Laurens Neinders (2023 University of Twente BSc thesis, "Improving Trackmania Reinforcement Learning Performance: A Comparison of Sophy and Trackmania AI").** Diagnosed that TMRL's LIDAR-only observation could plan for the next turn but not two turns ahead, whereas GT Sophy sees track curvature up to ~6 seconds ahead. He added a **Sophy-style track-curvature lookahead**: from LIDAR he fit a pair of Bézier curves to the two track borders and fed a segment of that curve to the network. Result: improvement — the agent approached average-player time on tmrl-test (still ~4 s off the human record). **Directly relevant:** curvature/centerline lookahead is the highest-leverage observation upgrade, and in PolyTrack you can compute it exactly from the track code rather than estimating from LIDAR.

**Rottaca (SelfDrivingConvNet, 2017).** Earliest known TMNF self-driver: supervised behavioural cloning of ~100,000 labelled screenshots (left/right/neither), no braking, no momentum concept; the CNN learned turn vs straight but eventually crashed. Illustrates the ceiling of pure pixel behavioural cloning — the same ceiling PILA and Polybot hit in PolyTrack.

**Other TMNF/TM2020 hobby projects** documented by the "Hall of Impossible Dreams" blog series (hallofdreams.org/posts/trackmania-1, -2, -3): **AndrejGobeX/TrackMania_AI**, **LouisDeOliveira/TMAI**, **PedroAI** (DQN with 4 workers — 3 collectors + 1 learner, cycling maps each session for generalisation, streamed on Twitch), and **Bluemax666**. The blog trilogy chronicles the whole history plus the authors' own attempt, which was dominated not by ML but by the engineering nightmare of instrumenting a closed real-time game (installation, screen capture, speed-up). Their fast screenshot approach (tiny grayscale frames held in RAM) and struggle motivates the PolyTrack headless-simulator strategy.

**IEEE 2025 paper "Reinforcement Learning in TrackMania"** (2025 5th International Conference on Computer Science, Electronic Information Engineering and Intelligent Control Technology (CEI); ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11398478/). Trained Deep Q-Learning agents in TMNF; reported lap times "comparable to those of human experts" and, importantly, **generalisation to unfamiliar courses** and consistency exceeding human players. Confirms discrete DQN-family methods generalise across tracks when trained to.

**TMInterface (donadigo & Kim / Trackmania Interface team).** A TAS tool for TMNF that provides: programmatic input injection, deterministic vehicle control, full simulation/car-state access, **save states**, screenshot capture (direct surface capture), menu navigation/cutscene skipping, and **game-speed modification** (the single most important feature for RL throughput — "playing the game dozens of times faster or slower than normal"). donadigo worked directly with the Linesight devs to expose these APIs. **What a PolyTrack equivalent must replicate:** deterministic step control, save/load state, headless stepping decoupled from wall-clock (i.e., run the physics as fast as CPU allows), full car-state readout (position, velocity in car frame, wheel contact, checkpoint index), and reset — all achievable by loading the PolyTrack WASM/C++ physics module directly rather than screen-scraping the browser.

### 2. Academic literature on racing RL

**GT Sophy — Wurman et al., "Outracing champion Gran Turismo drivers with deep reinforcement learning," Nature 602:223–228 (2022).** The landmark result: superhuman wheel-to-wheel racing in GT Sport.
- **Algorithm:** QR-SAC (Quantile-Regression Soft Actor-Critic) — a distributional extension of SAC — trained asynchronously and distributed (PS4 consoles collecting, cloud learner).
- **Observations:** car-local linear velocity/acceleration, and crucially **course points**. Per Peter Wurman's GTPlanet interview, "The track ahead of Sophy is divided into 60 equally-spaced segments, with the length of each segment calculated dynamically by the car's speed. Each segment represents approximately the next 6 seconds of travel at any given time." Left edge, centerline and right edge are each represented this way — the canonical curvature-lookahead design.
- **Reward:** dense course-progress reward plus penalties for wall contact and off-course (>2 tires off), plus event terms for overtaking/collisions in the racing task. Ablations in the paper confirm the QR enhancement, the point-sequence track representation, the off-course penalty, and a 7-step return were each necessary; per the paper, GT Sophy "demonstrates high performance in outracing human players with only 24 hours of QR-SAC training" on Maggiore (the track where its margin over humans was smallest).
- **Relevance:** the reward + course-point lookahead recipe is the gold standard; PolyTrack can compute exact course points from the track code.

**Fuchs et al., "Super-Human Performance in Gran Turismo Sport Using Deep Reinforcement Learning," IEEE RA-L 6(3):4257–4264 (2021); arXiv:2008.07971.** Time-trial superhuman agent using **SAC** and, per the abstract, "a high-fidelity physical car simulation, a course-progress proxy reward, and deep reinforcement learning." Observations are rangefinder measurements + car velocity/acceleration. Per the paper, "We use a distributed sampling scheme to collect samples from 4 PlayStation 4 simulating 20 cars each, and then, store the sampled trajectory in a fixed size first in, first out (FIFO) replay buffer"; the resulting policy outperformed the fastest known times in a dataset of personal-best laps from over 50,000 human drivers. The direct predecessor of Sophy and a clean template for the time-trial (not wheel-to-wheel) problem PolyTrack actually is.

**Vasco et al. / Lee et al. vision-based GT agents.** "A Super-human Vision-based RL Agent for Autonomous Racing in Gran Turismo" (RLC 2024; arXiv:2406.12563) uses an **asymmetric actor-critic** (critic gets global features, actor gets images) with QR-SAC to reach Sophy-comparable time-trial performance from images alone. "A Champion-level Vision-based RL Agent for Competitive Racing in Gran Turismo 7" (arXiv:2504.09021, IEEE RA-L 2025) extends this to GT7 wheel-to-wheel. Relevance: shows vision-only is possible but harder; strong argument for using structured state (from the simulator) rather than pixels in PolyTrack.

**Song et al., "Autonomous Overtaking in Gran Turismo Sport Using Curriculum Reinforcement Learning," ICRA 2021.** Curriculum RL for overtaking. Relevance for PolyTrack is mainly the curriculum idea (progress from easy to hard tracks), since PolyTrack time-trial has no opponents.

**OfflineMania — Macaluso, Sestini, Bagdanov (SEED/EA & Univ. Florence), arXiv:2407.09415 (2024).** A TrackMania-inspired **Unity** single-agent racing environment with a **Gymnasium interface** and ML-Agents, purpose-built as an **offline-RL benchmark**, shipping datasets of varying quality (random→expert) and sizes (e.g., a "basic_small" 5,000-transition set) plus baselines for online RL, offline RL, and offline-to-online fine-tuning. Uses a **track-centerline reward**. Relevance: if you collect human PolyTrack replays (they are downloadable via the recordings API), offline RL / offline-to-online is a viable warm-start; OfflineMania is the closest published methodology.

**Formula RL — Remonda et al., arXiv:2104.11106 (2021).** Deep RL for autonomous racing from **telemetry data** (not pixels), studying multi-action stability. **Simulation benchmark with large-scale human data — Remonda et al., arXiv:2407.16680 (2024).** A racing-simulation benchmark shipping large-scale human driving data; useful as a reference for imitation/offline warm-starts and for how human data is structured for racing RL.

**F1TENTH (O'Kelly et al., NeurIPS 2019 competition track) and the unifying survey (Evans et al., arXiv:2402.18558, 2024).** The main scaled-autonomous-racing benchmark. The survey benchmarks classical pipelines (particle-filter localisation, minimum-curvature trajectory optimisation + tracking, MPCC, follow-the-gap) against end-to-end RL and finds **optimisation-and-tracking achieves the fastest laps**, with online planning second — a strong signal that for pure lap-time, trajectory optimisation is competitive with or beats learning. Related: trajectory-aided learning (racing-line-in-the-loop RL, TD3) and model-based world-model RL (Dreamer-style) outperforming model-free on F1TENTH time-lap generalisation.

**MOSEAC / "Reinforcement Learning with Elastic Time Steps," Wang & Beltrame, arXiv:2402.14961 (RLC 2024; Polytechnique Montréal).** An off-policy actor-critic (extending SAC) with **elastic time steps** that dynamically adjusts control frequency to the lowest viable rate, adding a time dimension to the action space and an adaptive reward balancing task performance vs energy/step count. **Validated in TrackMania** (grayscale-CNN input), outperforming fixed-rate SAC/CTCO/SEAC on energy and completion. Relevance: for PolyTrack, variable action-repeat / frame-skip can cut environment steps substantially; even simple fixed frame-skip is worth tuning.

**Automated/coachable reward design for GT (arXiv:2511.02094; arXiv:2601.16906).** Recent Sony AI work automating GT reward design and learning driving styles from preferences; peripheral but confirms reward shaping remains the key lever and that a "production baseline GT Sophy" trains ~10 days on a single A100 + a dozen PS4s (~1500 epochs × 6000 steps).

### 3. Algorithm foundations relevant to PolyTrack (discrete keyboard actions)

PolyTrack uses discrete inputs (WASD/arrows; ~9 meaningful combinations of forward/back × left/straight/right, plus reset). This favours **discrete-action value-based and distributional methods**:
- **IQN (Dabney et al., arXiv:1806.06923).** Learns the full return quantile function via quantile regression with a random-tau cosine embedding; a flexible, SOTA distributional generalisation of DQN and the method Linesight actually uses. **Primary recommendation for the reactive policy.**
- **QR-DQN (arXiv:1710.10044) and C51** — precursors (fixed quantiles / categorical distribution). **Rainbow** — combines double-Q, dueling, **prioritized experience replay (PER)**, **n-step returns**, **NoisyNets**, and C51; a proven strong discrete-action baseline. For PolyTrack, an IQN-or-Rainbow core with PER + n-step is the sweet spot.
- **PPO / SAC (discrete SAC)** — on-policy PPO is simple, parallelises trivially across vectorised envs, and is robust; discrete SAC is an off-policy alternative. Good baselines but generally less sample-efficient per environment step than distributional DQN with PER for this kind of task; however, if your headless simulator is extremely fast, PPO's throughput can compensate.
- **Model-based / world models (DreamerV3, MuZero).** DreamerV3 offers strong sample efficiency and has shown model-based agents beat model-free on F1TENTH time-lap generalisation. **MuZero** learns a latent model and plans with MCTS — but note that in PolyTrack you already *have* the exact model, so you can run **planning directly on the true simulator** (AlphaZero-style with a perfect model) rather than learning one. This is a major PolyTrack advantage.

**Trajectory optimisation and search (likely to beat pure RL for per-track times):**
- **Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)** and **CMA-ES / evolution strategies** — optimise a fixed-length input sequence (or a small set of control waypoints) directly against the deterministic simulator's finish time. Simple, embarrassingly parallel, strong for open-loop TAS optimisation.
- **Beam search over input sequences** — expand the ~9-action tree frame-by-frame (or per action-block), scoring partial trajectories by progress/predicted time, keeping the top-k. Natural fit for a deterministic frame-stepped game and for stitching Yosh-style best micro-splits.
- **MCTS for deterministic single-agent optimisation** — UCT/best-first search over the exact model; combine with a learned policy/value net (AlphaZero-style) for guidance.
- **Hybrid "learned policy + test-time search"** — the pattern behind AlphaZero and, most recently, **"Superhuman AI for Stratego Using Self-Play RL and Test-Time Search" (Sokota et al., arXiv:2511.07312, 2025)**, which reached vastly superhuman Stratego for only a few thousand dollars by pairing self-play RL with test-time search. Lesson for PolyTrack: **learn a fast general policy, then spend test-time compute searching for the optimal per-track trajectory.**

**When search beats learning, and vice versa (for PolyTrack):**
- **Search wins** for *per-track time optimisation*: the model is exact, deterministic, cheap, and the objective (minimum finish time on a known track) is a fixed optimisation problem. CEM/beam/MCTS can exploit the exact model and find frame-perfect inputs a reactive net cannot.
- **Learning wins** for *generalisation and warm-starting*: a policy trained across many tracks completes an unseen track immediately and provides the priors/rollouts that make search tractable (good initialisation for CEM, action priors for MCTS/beam). It also handles the "discovery" problem (first completion on a new layout) where naive search from scratch is expensive.
- **Best: combine them** — policy for discovery + coarse line, search for the last tenths.

### 4. Reward shaping and observation design for racing
- **Reference-line / centerline progress reward** (Fuchs, Sophy, Linesight, OfflineMania) beats **sparse finish-time reward**: dense per-step progress along a reference path gives gradient everywhere and is the empirically dominant choice. Linesight defines a **reference line** the user specifies per map; progress along it over a short lookahead window is the reward.
- **A small per-frame time penalty** (or, equivalently, rewarding progress-per-time) incentivises speed; pure progress without a time term yields safe-but-slow driving (the TMRL failure mode).
- **Observation options, in order of leverage for PolyTrack:**
  - **Track-curvature / course-point lookahead** (Sophy; Neinders) — sample the centerline and edges ahead of the car in the car-local frame, range scaled by speed (Sophy uses 60 speed-scaled segments ≈ 6 s ahead). *Highest leverage; exactly computable from the PolyTrack track code.*
  - **Virtual LIDAR / raycasts** (TMRL, Fuchs) — cheap, generalises, but only sees the next turn. Useful as a complement or fallback.
  - **Raw pixels** (Rottaca, PILA, vision-based GT) — most general but least sample-efficient and hardest; unnecessary when you own the simulator.
  - **Velocity in the car-local frame**, **acceleration**, **wheel-contact/airborne state** (PolyTrack has loops and jumps, so airborne/contact state matters), **checkpoint-relative encoding** (index + relative position of next checkpoint), and **frame stacking / short history** (3–4 frames) for temporal context (velocity, spin).
- **Generalisation vs memorisation:** curvature-lookahead + car-local velocity + raycasts are **track-agnostic** and generalise; absolute position or track-ID encodings promote **per-track memorisation**. Use the former for the general policy; per-track fine-tuning/search handles memorisation implicitly.

### 5. Generalisation and procedural track generation
- Training across **many tracks** (PedroAI cycles maps; IEEE 2025 paper shows DQN generalising to unseen courses) prevents overfitting; PolyTrack's **built-in editor + exportable base62 "PolyTrack1…" codes** make an **infinite procedural training distribution** feasible — a decisive advantage over TrackMania.
- Literature: **procedural level generation for RL generalisation** (Justesen et al., "Illuminating Generalization in Deep RL through Procedural Level Generation," arXiv:1806.10729) shows PCG curricula substantially improve generalisation; **domain randomisation** (randomising track features/curvature/surface) is standard for zero-shot transfer; survey of zero-shot generalisation in RL (Kirk et al.). Combine with **curriculum** (easy→hard tracks) à la Song et al.
- Concretely: write a procedural PolyTrack track generator that emits valid track codes (straights, curves, loops, jumps, ramps of varying difficulty), sample a fresh distribution each episode, and hold out a validation set (the official campaign + community tracks) to measure generalisation.

### 6. PolyTrack-specific prior art and technical substrate

**Prior art (all early/limited):**
- **PILA — Polytrack Imitation Learning Agent (github.com/tryfonaskam/pila).** PyTorch (Apache-2.0, Python 3.11), **behavioural cloning from screen capture + recorded keys** (Shift/Ctrl/WASD/Q/E + mouse); records frames↔actions, trains a supervised net, runs real-time inference. Covered by Hackaday (28 June 2026). Limitation: pixel BC without temporal context and reward, so it inherits distribution-shift and cannot optimise time. Reusable: the data-capture/replay-input plumbing.
- **Polybot — Parth Vadia (github.com/ParthVadia13/polybot; Medium devlog, Oct 2025).** A GUI data-scraper / bot that pixel-scrapes the replay UI; the author reports it **failed on timing precision**. Reusable: pixel-grabbing and keystroke-replay utilities; cautionary tale about screen-scraping timing.
- **itch.io thread "Whats the view of TAS agents?" (Azi, created 26 Aug 2025).** Azi built a neural-net agent "not confined to one track," interfaced via **screen capture** (couldn't extract binaries via Cheat Engine, couldn't read speed via cv2), and rewarded/reset it **manually**; posted a working "TAS_Agent" replay code. Confirms the community interest and the exact pain points (no speed readout, no automated reward) that a headless simulator solves.
- **@PolyTAS (YouTube)** — a channel showcasing PolyTrack TAS runs.
- **beatrixwashere/polytracktas.** TAS resources: a **frame-indexed plaintext `.tas` format** (`frame,(wasd)` lines, e.g. `0,w` / `500,wd`) and **`tas_to_ghost.py`**, which encodes inputs into a **ghost** for playback; ghosts are stored in localStorage in the browser app. Also **PolyHack** mod loader (GameBanana) by the same author. **Directly reusable:** the `.tas` format and ghost encoder are exactly the output format your search/TAS pipeline should emit, and the ghost round-trips through the game for verification.
- **Hexcein-moonsters/polytrack, Density006/polytrack-for-google-sites** — static mirrors of the game "made for training AI" (note: CORS/leaderboard disabled). Points to a separate `polytrack_AI` repo as the "current state."

**Physics/determinism substrate:**
- **v0.4.0 (July 2024):** moved to "Wasm for physics simulation instead of asm.js," with multithreaded car simulation.
- **v0.6.0 (March 2026):** "rewriting the majority of the physics simulation in C++"; smaller track codes (rotation+rotationAxis combined into one byte); community tracks loaded on demand; added single-frame stepping in replays and an advanced graph window; added **`polytrackModConfiguration`** object (currently mod name/author; and unblocked builds must set `window.polytrackModConfiguration = { unblocked: true }`). A fixed "clz32 no deterministic implementation" error confirms determinism is actively enforced at the instruction level.
- **Determinism & verification:** the client runs a local **determinism self-test** (replay/asset) and refuses to submit to the backend unless determinism is OK; the changelog notes "Improved the determinism test to be more accurate to prevent invalid replays being submitted"; the server verifies replays and marks them Pending/Verified/Invalid/InvalidDuplicate/InvalidManual (there is a verifier role and a `/verifyRecordings` endpoint).
- **Recording format (reverse-engineered from the 0.5.2 client, htmlunblockedgames/polytrack-0.5.2-clean/API.md):** runs are recorded as the frame numbers where the five inputs (up/right/down/left/reset) toggle, delta-encoded, deflated, then URL-safe base64 encoded; **`frames` are physics frames at 60 Hz**; `Lv.maxFrames = 5,999,999`; uploads must be <10,000 encoded bytes. Backend at `https://vps.kodub.com/` with `GET/POST /leaderboard`, `GET /recordings`, `POST /verifyRecordings`, `GET/POST /user`. This means **you can download top human ghosts for imitation/offline warm-start and submit/verify programmatically** — subject to ToS.
- **Headless WASM stepping (public knowledge):** WASM modules can be loaded and stepped outside the browser via Node.js or standalone runtimes (wasmtime/wasmer); the Wasm-R3 record-reduce-replay work (arXiv:2409.00708) confirms standalone deterministic WASM replay is feasible, though **multithreading is the hardest case for deterministic replay** — relevant because PolyTrack's car simulation is multithreaded (you would likely run it single-threaded for determinism). No public, turnkey "headless PolyTrack physics" harness is documented yet; building one (extract the WASM/C++ physics module, feed it a track code and an input-per-frame stream, read car state each 60 Hz step) is the core engineering task and appears feasible but unproven.

**Kodub's Terms of Service / stance on bots, TAS, mods:** *This needs a direct primary-source check.* Observationally: the game tolerates a modding ecosystem (mod loaders, `polytrackModConfiguration`, unblocked-build flag), the community distinguishes TAS from human runs (speedrun.com tracks a "TAS?" flag), and Azi's stance ("I'm never going to pretend the agent is me or a real human player") reflects community norms. The **leaderboard enforces determinism and server-side replay verification and rejects invalid replays**, so TAS/bot runs that don't reproduce deterministically are auto-rejected; there is a distinct hacking-incident/verification history on speedrun.com. **You should not submit bot/TAS runs to the official human leaderboard** absent explicit permission (mirroring Linesight's own rule for TrackMania).

### 7. Practical engineering guidance
- **Throughput / parallelisation (the whole point of a headless simulator):**
  - **Vectorised environments / EnvPool-style batching:** run hundreds–thousands of PolyTrack physics instances in parallel processes/threads; because a step is cheap C++/WASM math, you can plausibly reach very high steps/s on one machine — orders of magnitude beyond TrackMania's real-time-bound ~1–10 FPS-per-instance.
  - **Distributed actor-learner:** **Ape-X** (distributed prioritized replay) pairs naturally with IQN/Rainbow; **IMPALA** (V-trace) with actor-critic/PPO. Linesight, PedroAI and Fuchs et al. all used multi-collector/single-learner designs — replicate this.
  - **Frame-skip / elastic time steps** (MOSEAC) to reduce decision frequency.
- **Sample budgets from comparable projects (calibration, not targets):** Yosh's A01 agent trained ~400 h (real-time, single track) to reach human-record level; TMRL's LIDAR policy trained ~200 h to reach robust-but-slow; Fuchs et al. used 4 PS4s (20 cars each) for its distributed sampling; a GT Sophy production baseline ≈10 days on one A100 + ~12 PS4s. In PolyTrack, because you can step far faster than real time, **wall-clock should be dramatically lower for equivalent environment steps** — the binding constraint shifts from game speed to your learner/GPU and simulator throughput.
- **Hardware:** a single modern workstation (one mid/high-end NVIDIA GPU + many CPU cores) is sufficient for a headless-sim project; Linesight targets ~20 GB RAM and one GPU.
- **Frameworks (recommendation):**
  - **CleanRL** — single-file, readable IQN/PPO/Rainbow implementations; best for a research-y project where you'll customise the algorithm and want to understand every line. **Top pick to start.**
  - **Stable-Baselines3** — batteries-included PPO/SAC/DQN with good docs; fastest path to a baseline, but discrete distributional methods (IQN) aren't first-class.
  - **Tianshou** — modular, has QR-DQN/IQN/Rainbow and good vectorised-env support; strong middle ground.
  - **Ray RLlib** — best if you need large-scale distributed Ape-X/IMPALA across machines; heavier to operate.
  - Verdict: **prototype in CleanRL or Tianshou (for native IQN/Rainbow + vectorised envs); scale with RLlib only if you outgrow one machine.** Wrap the headless simulator as a **Gymnasium** environment for compatibility with all of the above (as OfflineMania and TMRL do).

## What is genuinely novel about doing this in PolyTrack vs TrackMania
1. **You can own the model.** In TrackMania, every serious project (Linesight, TMRL, PedroAI, Hall of Dreams) burned most of its effort instrumenting a closed, real-time engine — screen capture, input injection, speed hacks (TMInterface). PolyTrack's physics is a portable WASM/C++ module with enforced determinism and 60 Hz frame stepping, so a **headless, save-state-capable, faster-than-real-time simulator is achievable**. This flips the project from "fight the game" to "exploit the model."
2. **Exact geometry, not estimated.** Sophy and Neinders had to estimate curvature lookahead (from global features or fitted Bézier curves). In PolyTrack you can compute the exact centerline/edges/curvature and checkpoint positions from the track code — the best-known observation for racing RL, for free.
3. **Infinite, controllable training distribution.** The editor + base62 track codes let you procedurally generate an unbounded curriculum for generalisation — something TrackMania projects approximated only by cycling a fixed map pool.
4. **True test-time search is practical.** Determinism + cheap stepping + save states make CEM/beam/MCTS over input sequences genuinely tractable, so the AlphaZero/Stratego "policy + test-time search" paradigm can be applied to a racing game in a way that is impractical in TrackMania.
5. **Verifiable output format exists.** The `.tas` → ghost pipeline and server-side deterministic verification give you a built-in oracle for correctness and a clean deliverable format.

## Recommended methods design for the PolyTrack agent
- **Simulator:** headless WASM/C++ physics core, single-threaded for determinism, exposed as a Gymnasium env with `reset(track_code)`, `step(action)`, save/load state.
- **Observation:** car-local velocity + acceleration; wheel-contact/airborne flags; 60-point speed-scaled centerline+edge lookahead computed from the track code (Sophy-style); a small raycast set as backup; next-checkpoint relative encoding; 3–4-frame history.
- **Action:** the ~9 discrete WASD combinations (+ reset for training only).
- **Reward:** centerline progress − small per-frame time penalty + terminal term rewarding low finish-frame count.
- **Algorithm:** IQN (or Rainbow) with PER + n-step, distributed collectors + single learner (Ape-X style), across a procedurally generated track curriculum with domain randomisation.
- **Optimisation layer:** per-track, warm-start from the general policy, then beam search / CEM / CMA-ES (optionally MCTS with the policy as prior) on the deterministic simulator to minimise finish frames, with Yosh-style segmented micro-split stitching; emit `.tas` and verify via the ghost encoder + determinism test.
- **Optional warm-start:** download top human ghosts and do behavioural cloning / offline RL (OfflineMania-style) to seed the policy and search priors.

## Recommendations (staged, with thresholds)

**Stage 0 — Build the headless deterministic simulator (highest priority, main risk).** Extract PolyTrack's WASM/C++ physics module and drive it outside the browser (Node.js or wasmtime/wasmer), single-threaded for determinism. Validate by reproducing a known ghost bit-for-bit (the determinism test + `.tas`/ghost round-trip via `tas_to_ghost.py` is your oracle). **Benchmark that changes the plan:** if you cannot achieve deterministic headless stepping, fall back to TMRL-style real-time browser control (screen capture + input injection + a JS console hook for speed/state), accepting ~1–10 FPS and much longer wall-clock.

**Stage 1 — Wrap as a Gymnasium env; baseline policy.** Implement the observation/action/reward above. Train IQN (CleanRL/Tianshou) with PER + n-step, or a PPO baseline for throughput. **Threshold to advance:** reliable completion (>95%) on unseen easy tracks.

**Stage 2 — Generalisation via procedural tracks + curriculum.** Build a procedural track-code generator; train on a randomised distribution with an easy→hard curriculum and domain randomisation. Validate on the official campaign + community tracks. **Threshold:** near-100% completion and human-competitive times across the held-out set (compare to speedrun.com/leaderboard times).

**Stage 3 — Per-track optimisation via test-time search (this is where you beat records).** For each target track, warm-start from the general policy, then run beam search / CEM / CMA-ES (and/or MCTS with the policy as prior) directly on the deterministic simulator to minimise finish frames; use segmented optimisation to stitch best micro-splits. Emit results in `.tas` format and verify via the ghost encoder + determinism test. **Threshold:** match, then beat, the best human ghost per track.

**Stage 4 — Warm-start and refinement (optional, parallelisable).** Download top human ghosts via the recordings API and use behavioural cloning / offline RL (OfflineMania-style) to warm-start the policy and seed search priors; add MOSEAC-style elastic time steps if step budget is the bottleneck.

**Governance:** treat official-leaderboard submission as **off-limits for bot/TAS runs** unless Kodub explicitly permits it; publish runs clearly labelled as TAS/AI (community norm), and confirm the current ToS position before any submission.

## Caveats
- **The headless-WASM simulator is unproven in public.** No turnkey "headless PolyTrack physics" harness is documented; multithreaded WASM complicates deterministic replay (run single-threaded). If extraction fails, throughput collapses to real-time browser control and the project's economics change substantially.
- **Several Linesight specifics are unverified.** Exact IQN hyperparameters (quantile counts, n-step, PER usage), current image resolution, full `state_float` semantics, reward coefficients, reference-line generation, action count, and training compute/GPU were not all confirmable in this review; the 160×120 grayscale and "7-second reference" figures come from a third-party history describing an earlier version. Read the repo's `config_files/config.py` and docs before copying numbers.
- **Media reports on Yosh's exact times differ** (23.75 / 23.72 / 23.64 s appear across secondary outlets for different stages of his project); treat specific hundredths as stage-dependent and verify against Yosh's own videos.
- **Kodub's explicit ToS text on bots/TAS/mods was not directly quoted here** — verify from the primary source (kodub.com / in-game / Discord) before relying on the "don't submit" guidance; it is inferred from the determinism/verification system and community norms.
- **PolyTrack physics changed between versions** (asm.js→WASM in v0.4.0; C++ rewrite in v0.6.0; track-code format change). Pin a specific game version for your simulator and training, since replays/ghosts and track codes are version-sensitive and the leaderboard groups tracks by release version.
- **Search-vs-learning boundary is empirical.** Whether beam/CEM/MCTS beats a well-tuned per-track-fine-tuned policy at the last hundredths depends on action-block granularity and horizon; budget experimentation for this.
- **Reverse-engineered API details (0.5.2) may be stale** for v0.6.0, and bulk ghost download may itself be restricted by ToS/rate limits.
